WheresMyWater.org

Founder · Jul 2026 – Present · Scottsdale, AZ

Built after the Rio Verde Foothills water crisis left hundreds of Arizona families searching for answers. Enter any Arizona address and WheresMyWater.org shows your water provider, nearby water haulers, and other resources in one place.

The free tool pulls together public data from state and federal sources so people can find help before an emergency. It reached 28,840 unique visitors in the last 30 days and is now expanding to the rest of the United States.

A Month of Hard Labor in Peru

Brophy Immersion & Exchange · May – June 2026

I spent four weeks in Peru on Brophy's longest immersion and its only exchange program: 24 students and three teachers working alongside Jesuit partners from Lima to Arequipa, through the Andean villages outside Cusco, and up to Machu Picchu.

Almost every day was hard physical labor: breaking rock with sledgehammers to dig ditches, pouring a concrete classroom wall, painting chapels, and carving a staircase up a mountainside. In Arequipa we lived with Peruvian host families, who became a second family.

Working beside communities with far harder water access than Arizona stayed with me. A month after I got home, I launched WheresMyWater.org.

Vigil — NASA Space Apps Challenge

Team Leader · The Trojans · Phoenix, AZ

I led Team Trojans at NASA's Space Apps Challenge, where our challenge was to build health-monitoring software for astronauts on long missions. We built Vigil, an offline health monitor that learns each crew member's own normal from their first ten days of readings, then watches heart, immune, bone and muscle, behavior, and radiation data for slow drift.

Vigil combines small changes across many signals into one score, explains every alert in plain words with the numbers behind it, and decides what happens next: handle it on board, tell the Medical Officer, or send a priority packet to Earth across a 22-minute communication delay.

It's grounded in real NASA science. A clickable body map ties every reading to NASA's five hazards of human spaceflight, cabin CO2 and radiation dose are judged against NASA-STD-3001 limits, and real data on 167 solar storms from NASA's DONKI database drives the radiation screen. It runs as a native desktop app with no internet connection, and every astronaut signs in with a PIN and chooses what to share.

To make daily check-ins something astronauts actually want to do, we added an organ group chat, texts from future you, a heart rate album, and Gut City.
